# Synthesis notes week 1 - Law and ethics 


## Week 2 – Fairness, predictive systems & data justice

- Predictive tools (e.g. PredPol, risk scores) raise deep questions about **fairness** and discrimination.
- Formal fairness notions can be defined over:
  - Predicted labels alone.
  - Predicted vs actual outcomes.
  - Risk scores vs actual outcomes.
- Different fairness definitions can conflict, so “fair by the numbers” is not straightforward.

**Data justice (Linnet Taylor):**

To rebalance power in datafied societies, systems should ensure:

- **Visibility** – ability to be represented when needed and to opt out of data collection.
- **Autonomy** – control over how one engages with public and private data markets.
- **Non‑discrimination** – ability to challenge biased data/decisions and avoid unfair profiling.

Implied rights:

- Access to data collected about oneself.
- Ability to query and contest data and outputs.
- Mechanisms to hold governments and companies to account.

Some groups (e.g. Liberty in the UK) argue that certain systems, such as predictive policing, are structurally harmful and should be banned rather than “repaired”.
