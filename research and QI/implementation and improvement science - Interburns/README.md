# Implementation & Improvement Science – Interburns

Short course on quality improvement, implementation, and value‑based healthcare in burn services, with tools that generalise to wider clinical settings.


# summary and synthesis notes

## Core ideas

- Aim of QI: strengthen services and improve care so it is **safe, timely, effective, efficient, patient‑centred and equitable**.
- Any project should ask:
  - Will it make the service run more smoothly with the resources we have?
  - Will it improve outcomes?
  - Does it put anyone at risk?
  - Is it feasible in the timeframe?
  - Does it prioritise those most in need?

## QI questions & methods

- Three core QI questions:
  1. What are you going to improve, and by how much?
  2. How will you know a change is an improvement (what can you measure reliably)?
  3. What changes can you make that will lead to improvement?
- PDSA cycle (Plan–Do–Study–Act): test changes in small steps, then adapt, adopt, or discard; be aware of innovation fatigue and competing priorities.
- Tools to understand problems:
  - Process mapping, SWOT analysis, Pareto (80/20), driver diagrams, scatter plots, fishbone (Ishikawa), five whys.
  - SMART aims: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Timely.

## Value-based and prudent healthcare

- Cost‑effectiveness: compare two treatments in terms of costs (direct, indirect, intangible) and outcomes.
- Prudent/value‑based healthcare:
  - Co‑production with patients and public.
  - Care for those in greatest need first.
  - Do only what is needed and do no harm.
  - Reduce inappropriate variation via evidence‑based care.
- Value = outcomes that matter to patients divided by cost of delivering them; link to ICHOM outcome sets.

## Change, behaviour and teams

- Good projects are **realistic and feasible**, use available resources, and start with small, well‑done steps; team engagement is critical for sustainability.
- Team questions: who leads, who is affected, who brings clinical/technical expertise, who sponsors/supports, how often and how you communicate.
- Knowledge‑to‑action pipeline: awareness → acceptance → applicability → ability → action → agreement → adherence; behaviour change frameworks (e.g. Behaviour Change Wheel: capability, opportunity, motivation) help design interventions.

## My key takeaway

Improvement science is structured common sense: define a clear, measurable aim, understand the system with simple tools, run small PDSA cycles, and keep value‑based, patient‑centred outcomes at the core.[file:320]
