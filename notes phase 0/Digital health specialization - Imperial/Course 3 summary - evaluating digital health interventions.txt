# Digital Health (Imperial, Course 3) – Evaluating Digital Health Interventions

## Week 1 – Data for digital health

- Data is the **currency** of digital health: tools collect, curate and analyse data, so gaps directly affect equity and impact.
- Current problems: lack of primary data, poor coordination between systems, delays in availability, data that doesn’t match end‑user needs.
- Good data should be timely, policy‑relevant, reliable, privacy‑preserving and securely stored; actions include gap assessments, standardisation and better coordination.

### Statistical basics for reading studies

- Check whether groups are comparable at baseline (age, sex, key clinical variables).
- Understand parametric vs non‑parametric tests; choose tests appropriate to data type and distribution.
- Ensure correct use of paired tests, two‑tailed tests where harms are possible, and thoughtful handling of outliers.
- Data management: validation (internal/external), cleaning, descriptive statistics, then appropriate models (linear, logistic, Poisson).

## Week 4 – Frameworks and models for evaluation

- Use **complex intervention frameworks** (e.g. MRC): development → evaluation → implementation.
- Key evaluation functions: assess effectiveness, understand mechanisms of change, and assess cost‑effectiveness.
- Robust trials (e.g. TASMINH4) require pre‑specified analysis, adequate sample size, clear inclusion criteria, documented withdrawals and appropriate sensitivity analyses.

### Persuasive system design & logic models

- Persuasive system design (Oinas‑Kukkonen): 3 phases – understand key issues & postulates, analyse persuasion context (intent, event, strategy), translate insights into design features.
  - Design support categories: primary task support, dialogue support (feedback), credibility, social support.
- Logic models: map structure → processes → outcomes; specify context, resources, activities, short‑ and long‑term outcomes, and indicators.
  - Include **dark logic models** to think about unintended consequences.

### Statistical analysis plans (SAPs)

- SAP = detailed technical plan of analyses before data are seen; should be finalised and ideally published before unblinding.
- Benefits: guides analysis/reporting, aligns with protocol, reduces bias, improves transparency, supports reproducibility.
- Key elements: objectives & hypotheses, study design, primary/secondary outcomes, sample size, analysis methods.

## My key takeaway

Rigorous evaluation of digital health needs three things together: **good data**, theory‑driven design (logic models, PSD) and pre‑specified, transparent analysis plans—not just impressive apps.
