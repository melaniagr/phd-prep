# Digital Health (Imperial, Course 2) – Summary

## When digital health projects succeed

Five contextual enablers increase the chance a digital health project will work: 

- Supportive regulatory environment.
- Culture of innovation.
- Leadership committed to clinical quality and public health.
- Strong data governance.
- Public support and trust.

Estonia’s national e‑health system is a flagship example: a highly digital society, unified infrastructure, and continuous improvement around policy, services and tech.

## Why many digital health projects fail

Common reasons digital health start‑ups and interventions are “not fit for purpose”:

1. Ignoring patients and diversity.
2. Ignoring care providers.
3. Weak or unclear clinical value.
4. No scientific validation.
5. Overclaiming what the tech can do.
6. Not going where users are.
7. Poor cost‑effectiveness.
8. Hard to implement in real clinical workflows or for patients.
9. Not matching the slow pace of healthcare change.
10. Launching too early or too late.

## Evidence on impact (EHR‑centred)

- There is heavy global interest in EHRs and other digital tools to transform care, but **robust evidence is often missing** for outcomes and cost‑effectiveness; risks are under‑studied.
- Systematic overview (Black et al. 2011) grouped technologies as:
  1. Data storage/management/transmission (e.g. EHRs).
  2. Clinical decision support.
  3. Remote care.
- Many claimed benefits (quality, safety, efficiency) are repeated without strong evidence, highlighting the need for rigorous evaluation and cautious implementation.
